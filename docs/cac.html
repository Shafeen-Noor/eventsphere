1. Executive Summary

EventSphere is a pay-per-event digital experience platform: one QR code, one hub for photos, guestbook, polls, and host controls. Revenue is collected once per event, not via monthly SaaS subscriptions. This report quantifies the cost to acquire each paying host under EventSphere's launch go-to-market strategy, which deliberately excludes a free tier and relies on two complementary acquisition channels.

The partnership channel is the core engine. Event vendors — photographers, planners, venues, and DJs — already stand beside hosts at live events. They pitch EventSphere in context, earning a 20% commission only after a successful sale. At modeled scale (30 active partners), this channel produces 60 paying events per month at a blended CAC of $6.40, with contribution profit of $1,297 and a 68% contribution margin. There is no upfront cash outlay for acquisition; commission is variable and tied directly to revenue.

The paid advertising channel serves as a controlled backup and demand-generation lever. A $1,000 monthly test budget yields 45 paying events at $22 CAC. The channel is profitable in aggregate (+$221 contribution profit, 16% margin) but structurally dependent on Premium plan mix: Essential events lose $5.47 per unit after ads CAC, while Premium events contribute +$11.85. Creative and targeting must therefore skew toward higher-value events (weddings, corporate galas, large celebrations).

Combined, both channels deliver 105 paying events, $3,312 gross revenue, and $1,518 contribution profit in a representative launch month. Blended CAC is $13.18 against a blended gross margin of approximately $28.01 per event (weighted by plan mix), implying strong first-event economics and a healthy foundation for LTV analysis (see companion LTV report).

Investment thesis in one line: Partnerships deliver capital-efficient, high-margin acquisition with no upfront spend; paid ads extend reach when Premium-heavy, but should not carry Essential-heavy volume at current price points and funnel conversion rates.

2. Scope, Methodology & Definitions

This document covers customer acquisition cost for EventSphere's paid-only launch model as of August 2026. It does not model Enterprise contracts (from $199/month), future price changes, or geographic expansion. All currency is USD. Time horizon is one representative launch month unless otherwise stated.

2.1 Scope boundaries

  • In scope: Essential ($19) and Premium ($39) per-event plans; Stripe payment processing; AWS infrastructure COGS; two acquisition channels (partnerships, paid ads).
  • Out of scope: Customer support headcount, engineering salaries, office overhead, product development capex, tax, and multi-month cohort retention (addressed separately in LTV analysis).
  • Customer definition: A "paying event" is one host who completes checkout for a single event. Repeat purchases by the same host are modeled in LTV, not here.
  • GTM model: Paid-only — hosts encounter limits during event creation and must upgrade to publish. There is no permanently free tier.

2.2 Methodology

Unit economics are built bottom-up from list price, payment processing fees, and estimated AWS cost per event. Channel P&L statements apply funnel conversion assumptions to derive customer counts, then multiply by per-plan economics. Combined figures are simple sums across channels with no deduplication (channels are assumed to reach distinct customer segments in month one).

Sensitivity tables vary one input at a time while holding others constant, to isolate drivers of CAC and contribution margin. All intermediate funnel steps are shown so investors can audit arithmetic independently.

2.3 Definitions

Term Definition Formula / note
CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost) Total acquisition spend divided by number of new paying events acquired in the period. CAC = Acquisition cost ÷ Paying events
COGS (Cost of Goods Sold) Direct variable cost to deliver one paid event: Stripe fees + AWS infrastructure (storage, compute, egress). Excluded from CAC; deducted from revenue before contribution profit
Gross margin (GM) Revenue minus COGS for one event, before any acquisition cost. GM = Price − Stripe − AWS
Contribution profit Revenue minus COGS minus acquisition cost for a cohort or channel. Also called contribution margin in dollar terms
Contribution margin % Contribution profit as a percentage of gross revenue. Contribution profit ÷ Revenue
Blended CAC Weighted average CAC across channels by customer count. Total acquisition ÷ Total paying events
Partner commission 20% of gross transaction value, paid to referring vendor after successful checkout. Treated as variable CAC for partnership channel
Pay-to-publish Conversion from partner pitch to completed payment. Modeled at 67% for warm in-person introductions

2.4 Reporting conventions

Percentages are rounded to whole numbers unless precision matters for funnel math. Dollar figures use two decimal places for per-unit metrics and whole dollars for channel totals where appropriate. Plan mix is expressed as Essential / Premium (e.g., 35/65 means 35% Essential, 65% Premium). Positive contribution is shown in green; negative per-unit outcomes after ads CAC are flagged in red.

Backup channel

5.2 Conversion rate explanations

  • CTR (2.5%): Event-planning audiences on Meta and Google respond to visual creative (QR hub, live feed screenshots). 2.5% is conservative for retargeting and achievable for interest-based prospecting with strong creative.
  • Click-to-checkout-start (9%): Not every click enters the create flow. Some bounce from landing page load time, mismatch of ad promise, or mobile friction. 9% reflects a focused landing page with a single CTA.
  • Checkout-to-payment (50%): Hosts who start checkout have intent but may abandon at plan selection, price shock, or payment form. Paid-only model means no "save for later" free tier — 50% completion is realistic for cold traffic.
  • Compound click-to-buy (4.5%): 1,000 × 4.5% = 45 paying customers. This is the single metric that determines CAC at fixed budget.

6.1 Blended unit economics

Combined plan mix across 105 events is approximately 37% Essential (39 events) and 63% Premium (66 events). Blended gross revenue per event is $3,312 ÷ 105 = $31.54. Blended acquisition cost per event is $1,384 ÷ 105 = $13.18. After COGS (~$3.90 blended), contribution profit per event is approximately $14.46.

Blended CAC = ($384 + $1,000) ÷ (60 + 45) = $1,384 ÷ 105 = $13.18
Combined contribution margin = $1,518 ÷ $3,312 = 45.8% ≈ 46%

6.2 Channel allocation guidance

  • Prioritize partnerships until partner density saturates target metros — higher margin, zero upfront CAC, stronger trust transfer.
  • Run ads as a laboratory — $1,000/month cap, Premium-weighted creative, strict ROAS monitoring by plan tier.
  • Do not scale ads into Essential-heavy traffic without either improving click-to-buy above 6% or reducing CPC below $0.85.
  • Reinvest contribution profit from partnerships into partner success (collateral, co-marketing) before increasing ad budget.

9. Risks & Limitations

9.1 Model limitations

  • Single-month snapshot: This report models one representative month, not a ramp curve or seasonal adjustment (wedding seasonality, holiday parties).
  • No overlap deduplication: Combined totals assume partner and ad channels reach distinct customers. In practice, some hosts may encounter both a vendor pitch and an ad.
  • Excludes fixed opex: Engineering, support, and G&A are not allocated. Contribution profit is not net profit.
  • Simulated payments: MVP currently simulates checkout; actual Stripe conversion and chargeback rates may differ slightly.
  • AWS estimates: Per-event infrastructure cost will shift with usage patterns, image compression, and retention extensions.

9.2 Partnership risks

  • Partner activation decay: Pitch rate may fall below 60% without ongoing partner success management and incentive programs.
  • Commission pressure: Competitors or large vendors may negotiate above 20%, compressing margin.
  • Geographic concentration: 30 partners in one metro does not generalize nationally without replication cost.
  • Quality variance: A poorly pitched referral converts below 67% and can damage brand perception.

9.3 Paid ads risks

  • Essential drag: At $22 CAC, Essential events lose $5.47 each — scaling ads without Premium skew destroys value.
  • CPC inflation: Wedding and event keywords are seasonal and competitive; $1 CPC may rise in peak season.
  • Funnel fragility: Click-to-buy at 4.5% is sensitive to landing page changes, mobile UX, and payment friction.
  • Attribution error: Multi-touch journeys (ad → research → partner referral) are not modeled.

9.4 Strategic risks

  • Paid-only friction: Requiring payment before publish may reduce top-of-funnel volume vs. freemium competitors; conversion assumptions must hold.
  • Per-event pricing ceiling: Hosts who run many events may eventually demand subscription pricing, changing unit economics.
  • LTV dependency: First-event contribution is strong for partners, but long-term value requires repeat events (see LTV report).

10. Appendix — Formulas

All calculations in this report derive from the formulas below. Variables are defined in Section 2.

10.1 Unit economics

Stripe COGS     = (Price × 0.029) + 0.30
AWS COGS        = S3 + CDN + Compute   (see P-06, P-07)
Gross margin    = Price − Stripe COGS − AWS COGS
Partner comm    = Price × 0.20
Profit|partner  = Gross margin − Partner comm
Profit|ads      = Gross margin − Ads CAC

10.2 Partnership funnel

Events touched    = Partners × Events_per_partner
Pitches           = Events_touched × Pitch_rate
Paying events     = Pitches × Pay_rate
Customers/partner = Paying events ÷ Partners

Partner revenue   = (N_essential × $19) + (N_premium × $39)
Commission        = Partner revenue × 0.20
Partner CAC       = Commission ÷ Paying events
Partner profit    = Partner revenue − Commission − COGS

10.3 Paid ads funnel

Clicks            = Impressions × CTR
CPC               = Budget ÷ Clicks
Checkout starts   = Clicks × Click_to_checkout_rate
Paying events     = Checkout starts × Payment_completion_rate
Click_to_buy      = Click_to_checkout_rate × Payment_completion_rate
Ads CAC           = Budget ÷ Paying events
Ads profit        = Revenue − Budget − COGS

10.4 Combined metrics

Total events      = Partner events + Ads events
Total revenue     = Partner revenue + Ads revenue
Total acquisition = Commission + Ad spend
Blended CAC       = Total acquisition ÷ Total events
Combined profit   = Partner profit + Ads profit
Combined margin % = Combined profit ÷ Total revenue

10.5 Worked example — blended CAC

Partners: 60 events, $384 acquisition
Ads: 45 events, $1,000 acquisition

Blended CAC = ($384 + $1,000) ÷ (60 + 45)
= $1,384 ÷ 105
= $13.18 per paying event
