01 Executive Summary

EventSphere monetizes through a pay-per-event model: hosts purchase a one-time license to publish a guest-facing event page, upload media, and share a link. There is no recurring subscription; revenue scales with the number of events a customer hosts over time, not with monthly active users alone.

Under base-case assumptions — a blended gross margin of $28.01 per event, a conservative repeat curve totaling 1.70 events per host over three years, and channel-weighted acquisition costs from our CAC model — EventSphere delivers attractive unit economics on its primary partnership channel and acceptable blended returns when paid advertising is used as a controlled supplement.

Investment thesis in one line: Partner-sourced hosts repay acquisition cost on the first event; every subsequent event in the relationship is predominantly gross margin. Repeat behavior — birthdays, client dinners, anniversaries, and professional referrals — is the primary lever that separates a single-transaction marketplace from a compounding customer asset.

02 How LTV is defined for pay-per-event (not SaaS MRR)

In subscription SaaS, lifetime value is commonly expressed as annual recurring revenue × gross margin % ÷ churn, producing a steady-state dollar figure tied to monthly billing. EventSphere does not fit that template: there is no monthly recurring charge, no seat count, and no automatic renewal. Each event is an independent purchase decision.

For EventSphere, we define LTV as follows:

LTVgross = Σ (eventst × blended gross margin per event)
LTVnet = LTVgross − CAC
LTV:CAC = LTVgross ÷ CAC

Key distinctions from SaaS LTV:

  • Event count, not tenure. The driver of value is how many events a host publishes, not how many months they remain "subscribed."
  • Variable purchase cadence. A wedding host may buy once; an event planner may buy monthly for client work.
  • No implied churn rate. We model explicit repeat probabilities by year rather than applying a single monthly churn constant.
  • Channel-specific CAC. Partnership commission is paid per first conversion; repeat events incur zero incremental acquisition cost under the current partner structure.
Concept SaaS subscription EventSphere pay-per-event
Revenue unit Monthly / annual contract One-time per published event
LTV driver Retention × ARPU Repeat event frequency × margin per event
Expansion revenue Upsell tiers, seat adds Premium plan mix, additional events
Typical payback 6–18 months < 1 event (partner channel)
Healthy LTV:CAC 3×+ (VC benchmark) 3.6× blended · 7.4× partnerships (base case)

We report LTV at both 12-month and 3-year horizons. The 12-month figure reflects near-term cash recovery; the 3-year figure captures the full repeat tail assumed in our go-to-market planning.

04 Customer segments

LTV varies materially by who buys and why. We model three archetypes that map to our acquisition channels and repeat curves. The base-case blended figures in this report weight toward the partner channel (57% of volume), which skews toward Premium plans and professional adjacent hosts.

Segment Typical first event Plan mix 3-yr events Est. gross LTV Primary channel
Individual host Birthday, reunion, memorial 60% Essential 1.2 ~$30 Paid ads · word of mouth
Repeat host Wedding, milestone party 40% / 60% 1.7 $47.62 Partners · referrals
Planner / venue (via partner) Client wedding, corporate gala 20% / 80% 2.5–4.0 $70–$112 Partner referral · co-sell

Individual host

One-time or infrequent buyers who discover EventSphere through search ads or a friend's link. They choose Essential when guest counts are small. Repeat is driven by life events (annual birthday, family reunion every few years). Expected 3-year event count: 1.0–1.3.

Repeat host

Consumers who had a strong first experience — often a wedding or large celebration — and return for anniversaries, baby showers, or holiday gatherings. They upgrade to Premium when guest lists grow. This segment anchors our base-case 1.70 events / 3 years assumption.

Planner / venue via partner

Wedding photographers, DJs, planners, and venues recommend EventSphere at events they already serve. Each professional touchpoint can yield multiple client events per year. Partner commission (20%) is paid on the first conversion; subsequent events from the same host incur zero incremental CAC. This segment has the highest LTV upside and justifies partner recruitment investment.

05 Repeat purchase model — years 1, 2, and 3

Because EventSphere charges per event, "retention" manifests as repeat purchase probability — the likelihood that a host returns to buy another event license within a given period. We model additive events per host rather than a continuous subscription curve.

Period Incremental events / host Cumulative events Rationale
First purchase (Month 0) 1.00 1.00 100% of acquired hosts publish at least one event
Within 12 months +0.30 1.30 30% host a second event: birthday follow-up, client dinner, referral
Year 2 +0.25 1.55 Seasonal return; planners book next client season
Year 3 +0.15 1.70 Long-tail repeat hosts; declining marginal return

Why these rates are conservative

  1. Category behavior. Event hosting is episodic, not daily. A 30% second-event rate within 12 months exceeds typical one-and-done marketplace behavior (~15–20% in comparable consumer categories).
  2. Product stickiness. Hosts who uploaded photos and shared a link have sunk effort; switching cost for the next event is low (same account, saved templates) but nonzero.
  3. Partner reinforcement. Photographers and planners who introduced EventSphere at Event #1 are present at Event #2 and can re-pitch organically — without additional commission on the same host.
  4. Premium skew. Wedding and large-event hosts (65% Premium in partner channel) have higher celebration frequency in their social graph, increasing referral-driven repeat.

06 LTV calculation walkthrough

The following step-by-step derivation uses blended inputs from Section 3 and the repeat curve from Section 5. All figures are per acquired host unless noted.

Step 1 — Gross margin per event

Blended margin = (0.35 × $16.75) + (0.65 × $34.07)
                = $5.86 + $22.15
                = $28.01

Step 2 — Expected events over horizon

Events12mo = 1.00 + 0.30 = 1.30
Events3yr = 1.00 + 0.30 + 0.25 + 0.15 = 1.70

Step 3 — Gross LTV

LTVgross, 12mo = Events12mo × Blended margin
                      = 1.30 × $28.01 = $36.41

LTVgross, 3yr = Events3yr × Blended margin
                    = 1.70 × $28.01 = $47.62

Step 4 — Net LTV (after CAC)

Channel CAC Gross LTV (3yr) Net LTV (3yr) Calculation
Partnerships $6.40 $47.62 +$41.22 $47.62 − $6.40
Paid ads $22.22 $47.62 +$25.40 $47.62 − $22.22
Blended $13.18 $47.62 +$34.44 $47.62 − $13.18

Step 5 — Incremental value of repeat events

The first event contributes $28.01 in gross margin. Repeat events (0.70 additional over three years) contribute $28.01 × 0.70 = $19.61 — a 70% uplift over single-purchase LTV. On the partner channel, repeat margin is effectively commission-free, making the uplift nearly pure contribution profit.

10 Payback period analysis

Payback period measures how quickly gross margin from a new host recovers acquisition cost. For pay-per-event businesses, payback is naturally expressed in events rather than months.

Payback (events) = CAC ÷ Blended gross margin per event
Payback (months) ≈ Payback (events) ÷ (Events per host per month)
Channel CAC Margin / event Payback (events) Payback (time) Notes
Partnerships $6.40 $28.01 0.23 < 1 month First event repays CAC 4.4× over
Paid ads (blended) $22.22 $28.01 0.79 ~1 month Premium-only; Essential loses on event 1
Paid ads (Essential only) $22.22 $16.75 1.33 13+ months Requires repeat event to break even
Blended $13.18 $28.01 0.47 < 1 month Weighted toward partner speed

Partner-channel payback is effectively instantaneous: commission is deducted from the first transaction, and the remaining $21.61+ contribution per Essential event (or $26.27+ for Premium) flows immediately. This is structurally superior to SaaS models requiring 6–12 months of subscription receipts to recover CAC.

Cash timing advantage: Partner commission is paid after the customer pays EventSphere. There is no upfront CAC outlay — unlike paid ads, where $1,000 is spent before any conversion. See partnership funnel for cash-flow sequencing.

Comparable context

  • Event ticketing (Eventbrite, etc.). Take-rate models earn per ticket; repeat varies by organizer type. Professional organizers exhibit high repeat; consumer one-offs do not. EventSphere's partner channel mirrors the "pro organizer" path.
  • Photo sharing (SmugMug, Zenfolio). Subscription or per-gallery pricing with 60–80% gross margins. EventSphere's Premium tier at $39 with 87% margin is competitive; differentiation is event-native UX and partner distribution.
  • Wedding SaaS (The Knot, Zola). Freemium with vendor marketplace revenue. EventSphere avoids marketplace complexity; LTV is direct from host payment, simplifying unit economics.

13 Risks & limitations

This report presents a model, not observed cohort data. The following risks could materially affect realized LTV relative to projections.

Model limitations

  • No live cohort evidence. Repeat rates (0.30 / 0.25 / 0.15) are informed estimates, not measured retention curves. Post-launch cohort tracking is required to validate or revise.
  • Homogeneous blending. Per-host LTV varies by segment (Section 4); blended figures mask a long tail of one-time Essential buyers.
  • Static pricing. Model assumes no price changes, bundles, or promotional discounts that would alter margin per event.
  • Single geography. CAC and conversion assumptions reflect a US launch; international expansion may differ.

Downside risks

Risk Impact on LTV Mitigation
Lower repeat than modeled LTV:CAC approaches 3.4× (single-event floor) Partner re-engagement; email triggers at life-event intervals
Ads scale without Premium discipline Essential-heavy ads mix → negative first-event margin Wedding / large-event creative; cap ads at 40% of volume
Partner commission renegotiation Partner CAC rises; LTV:CAC compresses Volume tiers; exclusive territory deals; repeat-event exemption
COGS inflation (AWS, media storage) Blended margin falls below $28 Retention limits; tiered media caps; CDN optimization
Competitive free alternatives Repeat and Premium mix decline Partner distribution moat; event-native UX; pro workflow tools
Regulatory & platform risk: Payment processing and user-generated content policies (Stripe, app stores) are outside this model. A material policy change could affect conversion or COGS but is not quantified here.

14 Appendix — formulas

Reference definitions for all calculations used in this report.

Core LTV

Blended margin     = (f_E × margin_E) + (f_P × margin_P)
Gross LTV          = Events_horizon × Blended margin
Net LTV            = Gross LTV − CAC
LTV:CAC            = Gross LTV ÷ CAC

Plan-level margin

margin_plan        = price − stripe(price) − aws_plan
stripe(price)      = (price × 0.029) + 0.30
margin_Essential   = 19.00 − 0.85 − 1.40 = $16.75
margin_Premium     = 39.00 − 1.43 − 3.50 = $34.07

Blended inputs (partner-weighted)

f_E = 0.35,  f_P = 0.65
Blended price      = (0.35 × 19) + (0.65 × 39) = $32.00
Blended margin     = (0.35 × 16.75) + (0.65 × 34.07) = $28.01
Partner CAC        = (0.35 × 3.80) + (0.65 × 7.80) = $6.40

Repeat curve

Events_12mo        = 1.00 + 0.30 = 1.30
Events_3yr         = 1.00 + 0.30 + 0.25 + 0.15 = 1.70
LTV_gross_12mo     = 1.30 × 28.01 = $36.41
LTV_gross_3yr      = 1.70 × 28.01 = $47.62

Channel LTV:CAC (3-year)

Partner net LTV    = 47.62 − 6.40  = $41.22   →  7.4×
Ads net LTV        = 47.62 − 22.22 = $25.40   →  2.1×
Blended CAC        = (0.57 × 6.40) + (0.43 × 22.22) = $13.18
Blended net LTV    = 47.62 − 13.18 = $34.44   →  3.6×
